# Guided 1D DC Joule electrothermal study

WaveWorld's `/comsol` page guides one steady, one-dimensional DC Joule-heating study through the existing W-Sim native operation `sw-comsol-joule-heating-b` (`method-algo-comsol-joule-heating-1d-coupled`). The native implementation is pinned at W-Sim commit `dda2f6f1e1def3d9a49bea48c8766d222b2d8a14`; this workflow reuses that released operation without changing its source or contract. It is a bounded COMSOL-style example, not a COMSOL run or a claim of numerical parity.

## Model and inputs

The conductor is a uniform 1D segment of length `L` and constant cross-sectional area `area`. The end temperatures `T_0` and `T_L` are prescribed and fixed. Thermal conductivity `k` is constant. Electrical conductivity varies with temperature according to the native linear resistivity law:

`sigma(T) = sigma0 / (1 + alpha (T - T_ref))`

The electric and thermal fields satisfy `J = sigma E`, `E = -dV/dx`, `dJ/dx = 0`, and `-k d²T/dx² = qDot`, with `qDot = J E = J²/sigma(T)`. For a total current `I`, `J = I/area`; the native operation computes the potential drop and temperature field together. When `alpha = 0`, conductivity is constant and the temperature profile is the exact quadratic solution. For `alpha > 0`, the operation evaluates its closed-form coupled steady solution. It samples the fields at `N` evenly spaced positions, including both ends.

The guided native input has the exact operation keys `module`, `methodId`, `backend`, `mode`, the selected drive key, and `sigma0`, `alpha`, `T_ref`, `k`, `L`, `area`, `T_0`, `T_L`, and `N`. It sets `module = sw-comsol-joule-heating-b`, `methodId = method-algo-comsol-joule-heating-1d-coupled`, `backend = host`, and `mode = joule`. The form supplies one electrical drive: either total current `current` in amperes or voltage `voltage` in volts. The native contract rejects both drives supplied together and rejects negative drive values. If neither is supplied, the native default is `current = 10 A`. A voltage-driven case solves for the compatible current density by bisection. Other native defaults are `L = 0.05 m`, `area = 1e-6 m²`, `sigma0 = 5.8e7 S/m`, `alpha = 0.0039 K⁻¹`, `T_ref = 293.15 K`, `k = 400 W/(m·K)`, `T_0 = T_L = 293.15 K`, and `N = 5`. Native admission requires positive finite `L`, `area`, `k`, and `sigma0`; `alpha` and the selected drive must be finite and nonnegative; and `N` must be an integer from 2 through 4096. The guided form additionally requires current or voltage to be in `[0, 1e6]`, `sigma0` in `(0, 1e9]`, `alpha` in `[0, 1]`, `T_ref` in `(0, 1e5]`, `k` in `(0, 1e6]`, `L` in `(0, 1e4]`, `area` in `(0, 100]`, and both endpoint temperatures in `(0, 1e5]`.

## Authenticated native lifecycle and provenance

The guided form captures the submitted draft, maps it to the native input fields above, and creates one W-Sim `JobSpecV1` selecting module `sw-comsol-joule-heating-b`, method `method-algo-comsol-joule-heating-1d-coupled`, backend `host`, and mode `joule`. A logged-in user's bearer token is attached to the settlement client; the client requires an authenticated user before submitting the job to the W-Sim Host. The displayed result is accepted only after the returned job result is checked against the submitted input and native module/method/backend identity. Each result stays bound to the input snapshot that produced it; edits, reset, cancellation, or a superseding request invalidate stale work.

## Returned results

The native `result` reports the selected drive, solved current and voltage drop, current density, mean electric field, resistance and its nominal constant-conductivity reference, power, and power reconstructed from integrated generation. It also returns peak and mean volumetric generation, input material/geometry/boundary values, a sampled `Tmax` and its sampled position, the self-heating flag and coupling parameter, generation and conduction per-area totals, and their absolute energy-balance residual. `profile` contains the native arrays `x`, `T`, `V`, `sigma`, `E`, and `qDot`; potential is referenced to `V(0) = 0`, with the drop represented by a negative `V(L)`. `ok` is the native finite-value, conductivity, power-balance, energy-balance, and physical-branch check. Backend provenance is `id: w-sim-cuda-host`, `kind: host-f64`, and `deviceName: w-sim-cuda-host`.

The temperature maximum is the largest value among the returned `N` samples, not a separate continuous optimization. The energy-balance residual compares integrated Joule generation per unit area with conduction through the fixed-temperature ends; power consistency separately compares `I × voltageDrop` with integrated generation over the cross-section.

## Scope and limits

This is a steady DC 1D uniform-cross-section model with fixed end temperatures, constant `k`, and the stated `sigma(T)` law. It has no spatial variation of geometry or material law, contact or end resistance, radiation, thermoelectric/Peltier/Thomson effects, or transient heat-up. The temperature-dependent branch fails closed near its steady thermal-runaway limit and if the conductivity law becomes nonpositive in the domain. In one dimension, `area` is an effective cross-sectional parameter; the operation does not resolve a transverse electric or temperature distribution.

This workflow does not add a frontend solver, a 3D or transient solver, a general PDE framework, or COMSOL model-file support. It does not create, read, or execute `.mph` files, invoke COMSOL, or establish vendor parity. COMSOL documentation is cited only to define the conceptual reference: its Joule Heating interface couples Electric Currents with Heat Transfer in Solids, and the Electric Currents formulation notes the effective-area consideration for 1D models. These references do not validate this implementation against COMSOL: [Joule Heating interface](https://doc.comsol.com/6.3/doc/com.comsol.help.comsol/comsol_ref_heattransfer.30.15.html), [stationary studies and space dimensions](https://doc.comsol.com/6.3/doc/com.comsol.help.comsol/comsol_ref_environment.18.39.html), [Electric Currents formulation](https://doc.comsol.com/6.3/doc/com.comsol.help.acdc/comsol_ref_acdc.26.52.html), [temperature-dependent conductivity](https://doc.comsol.com/6.3/doc/com.comsol.help.acdc/comsol_ref_acdc.26.54.html), and [prescribed-temperature boundary condition](https://doc.comsol.com/6.3/doc/com.comsol.help.heat/heat_ug_ht_features.09.092.html).
